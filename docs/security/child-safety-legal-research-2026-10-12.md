# Child-safety and privacy obligations for a WA community netball club app

Research date: 12 October 2026. Sources were read live; where a primary page could not be fetched (sportintegrity.gov.au and esafety.gov.au returned 503 to every request from this environment; some WA Ombudsman and Netball Australia PDFs are image-only) this is flagged and secondary sources are named. ## 1. WA Working with Children (Screening) Act 2004 — LAW

**Instrument and status.** *Working with Children (Screening) Act 2004* (WA) (renamed from *Working with Children (Criminal Record Checking) Act 2004* by the *Working with Children (Criminal Record Checking) Amendment Act 2022*). Phase 1 of the 2022 amendments commenced **1 July 2023**. The *Working with Children (Screening) Amendment Bill 2025* passed Parliament **19 August 2026** and the amended Act took effect **17 September 2026** with no transition period (WA Government "Amendments to the Act" page, last updated 22 Sep 2026). "Phase 2" reforms were consulted on 26 Aug–23 Sep 2025 and remain under development; no bill yet (Communities page, updated 10 Feb 2026). One wa.gov.au page refers to a "2024" Act — that is a typo; the Act is the 2004 Act as amended.

**What changed 1 July 2023** (wa.gov.au announcement): more Class 1/2 offences that bar a card; people with a Negative Notice or Interim Negative Notice (INN) can no longer use the parent-volunteer or child-volunteer exemptions; better inter-agency information sharing; wider compliance powers for the WWC Screening Unit (entry warrants, inspections, document demands); cardholders/applicants must notify the Unit of changes to name, address, contact details or child-related work status.

**What changed 17 September 2026** (Factsheet NEG06, Sep 2026; "Amendments to the Act" page): "banned in one, banned in all". An *adverse interstate WWC decision* (interstate negative notice, interim bar/suspension, conditions, or assessment-based cancellation, s 8B) means the person cannot be issued or hold a WA card; the Unit must check the national WWC National Reference System on application and may use it for ongoing monitoring. Such people also lose access to the parent/child volunteer, under-18 student, short-term visitor and national-event exemptions. **Organisations:** "If an organisation becomes aware that an individual (including those accessing an exemption) is the subject of an adverse interstate WWC decision, they must immediately take steps to ensure the individual is removed from child-related work." Notices are also sent to the engaging organisation. Template ORG04 (volunteer declaration) was updated.

**Child-related work in sport.** Work is child-related if the "usual duties" involve or are likely to involve contact with a child in connection with a listed category. Relevant categories: **Category 12** "a club, association or movement (including of a cultural, recreational or sporting nature...) with a significant membership or involvement of children"; **Category 4** coaching/private tuition; **Category 15** overnight camp (no parent exemption). Official examples for Category 12: "coaches/tutors/instructors providing activities to children; referees/umpires/judges of junior teams or junior competitions" (Factsheet ORG13, Sep 2024). Committee members need a card only if their usual duties involve more-than-incidental contact with children; a board member who only attends meetings does not (ORG13 FAQ). Organisations cannot require a card from people who are exempt or not in child-related work — exempt people are *ineligible to apply* and the Unit "actively monitors" this (Factsheet CRW03, Aug 2026). WA Football's 2026 toolkit (secondary, sport-sector) recommends clubs still record a card *or* confirmation of exemption for all committee, coaches, assistant coaches, team managers, first-aiders and regular-contact volunteers.

**Parent volunteer exemption** (CRW03, Aug 2026). For Category 12: "A parent volunteering at the same club, association or movement at which their child is involved, or ordinarily involved." Applies only while the child is under 18; "parent" includes step-parent, de facto partner and a person with legal responsibility; grandparents, adult siblings, aunts/uncles are **not** covered and must hold a card. **No parent exemption for overnight camps** (Category 15). Under-18 volunteers are covered by the child volunteer exemption. A person with an INN, Negative Notice or adverse interstate decision cannot use any of these exemptions and "does not have to tell their organisation the reason" (CRW04, Sep 2026).

**Card validity and verification.** A WA WWC Check is valid for **three years** and covers all categories (wa.gov.au "What is the WWC Check", updated 20 Aug 2026). There is **no statutory duty to validate or register**, but the Unit's obligations page recommends keeping: a list of everyone in child-related work with application number, card number and expiry; copies of online card validations; copies of all Unit notifications (including INNs/Negative Notices) and actions taken. Online card validation and employer registration are available at workingwithchildren.wa.gov.au/online-services and /registeremployees; registration makes the Unit notify the organisation if a cardholder becomes ineligible, and lapses when the card is renewed (re-register). "No, an organisation does not have a legal obligation to check" whether a volunteer is prohibited from an exemption, but it is an offence to keep someone in child-related work once aware (CRW04).

**Interim Negative Notice.** Issued when the Unit's preliminary assessment indicates risk; the person must immediately stop child-related work and cannot use exemptions; "When you are notified that an employee or volunteer has been issued with an Interim Negative Notice or Negative Notice you must remove them from child-related work immediately" (ORG13).

**Offences for organisations** (Factsheet PEN02, Nov 2023; penalties are statutory maxima): engaging a person known to have a current Negative Notice/INN, or using the child/parent volunteer exemption for such a person — **$60,000 and 5 years' imprisonment** (s 22(3)); engaging a person known to have a pending Class 1/2 charge without a card — $60,000 / 5 years (s 22(2)); engaging a person in child-related work more than five days in a calendar year without a card or pending application — $12,000 / 12 months (s 22(6)); knowing the person withdrew an application — $12,000 / 12 months (s 22(5)).

**Could not verify:** the full Phase 2 proposal list (consultation paper not retrievable); whether workingwithchildren.wa.gov.au content differs from wa.gov.au (site blocked 403).

**What this means for a club app.** Store per-person role, WWC card number, expiry (3-year), validation date/evidence and exemption basis (parent of named registered junior; under-18 volunteer); flag expiry and remind to re-register with the Unit on renewal; record Unit notices and the date a person was removed from child-related roles; make "remove from all child-facing roles now" a one-click action available to admins; never let the app *require* a card from a parent-exempt volunteer, but capture a signed volunteer declaration (Template ORG04 wording, including the interstate-decision acknowledgement); treat overnight camps as a separate category where every adult needs a card.

## 2. WA Reportable Conduct Scheme — LAW (but community sport is NOT in scope)

**Instrument and status.** Part of the *Parliamentary Commissioner Act 1971* as amended by the *Parliamentary Commissioner Amendment (Reportable Conduct) Act 2022*; administered by Ombudsman WA. (Note: the task framed it under the Children and Community Services Act 2004 — that Act is referenced for definitions of "placement arrangement" but the scheme itself sits in the Parliamentary Commissioner Act.) Commenced **1 January 2023** (government agencies incl. local governments, child protection/OOHC, childcare, education, health, justice/detention); expanded **1 January 2024** (accommodation and residential services, religious bodies, disability services; plus "significant neglect" and "significant emotional or psychological harm" added as reportable conduct). Ombudsman Information Sheet 2 (March 2026) states this is now the complete list.

**Sporting clubs are not covered.** The March 2026 list of covered organisations contains no sport or recreation category. The only hook that could catch a netball club is "organisations that provide overnight camps for children as part of its primary activity" — a netball club's primary activity is netball, not camps. No source showed any announced phase to add community sport. Ombudsman WA has, however, been appointed (24 Oct 2025) to run a separate Child Safe Standards / Independent Oversight scheme that *will* apply to "organisations who engage in child-related work" (see section 7) — that is the likely future route by which sport comes under Ombudsman oversight.

**Scheme mechanics (for completeness / if a club becomes covered).** Reportable conduct: sexual offences, sexual misconduct, physical assault, other prescribed offences, significant neglect, behaviour causing significant emotional or psychological harm. "Employee" means persons over 18 including volunteers and contractors engaged to provide services to children. The **head of the organisation** must notify the Ombudsman **within 7 working days** of becoming aware of a reportable allegation or conviction (Information Sheet 4; FAQ March 2026), investigate (or appoint an investigator and tell the Ombudsman who), report the outcome "as soon as practicable", give the employee procedural fairness at three stages, and keep systems for reporting, notification, investigation, record-keeping and lawful disclosure. The scheme does not replace reporting to Police/Communities.

**What this means for a club app.** No reportable-conduct notification feature is legally required today. The design pattern (allegation log → head-of-organisation visibility → 7-working-day timer → investigation record → outcome report) is worth building as an internal "child-safety incident" register because Netball Australia policy already requires immediate referral of child-abuse matters and the forthcoming WA Child Safe Standards scheme will make complaint-handling and record-keeping auditable.

## 3. National Principles for Child Safe Organisations — GUIDANCE in WA today, becoming regulated

**Instrument.** Endorsed by COAG February 2019 (AHRC). Not legislated in WA; Netball WA's own page says "The principles are not currently mandatory." The ten principles (CCYP WA list, AHRC wording for 6, 8, 10):
1. Child safety and wellbeing is embedded in organisational leadership, governance and culture.
2. Children and young people are informed about their rights, participate in decisions affecting them and are taken seriously.
3. Families and communities are informed and involved in promoting child safety and wellbeing.
4. Equity is upheld and diverse needs respected in policy and practice.
5. People working with children and young people are suitable and supported to reflect child safety and wellbeing values in practice.
6. **Processes to respond to complaints and concerns are child focused.**
7. Staff and volunteers are equipped with the knowledge, skills and awareness to keep children and young people safe through ongoing education and training.
8. **Physical and online environments promote safety and wellbeing while minimising the opportunity for children and young people to be harmed.**
9. Implementation of the national child safe principles is regularly reviewed and improved.
10. **Policies and procedures document how the organisation is safe for children and young people.**
DSS guidance under Principle 8 asks whether the organisation has "mechanisms in place to monitor inappropriate use of ICT equipment or to ensure online interactions with children and young people are appropriate." (The AHRC site itself returned 403; wording taken from CCYP WA and the DSS guidance template.)

**WA framework.** Commissioner for Children and Young People WA publishes *Child Safe Organisations WA: Guidelines* (Nov 2019), a self-assessment tool, and parent/staff checklists, all mapped to the ten principles. WA Government "Statement of Commitment to Child Safety and Wellbeing" applies to agencies. On **24 October 2025** the WA Government named **Ombudsman WA** as the independent oversight body to "monitor and enforce compliance with the National Principles"; the Ombudsman's "Child Safe Standards" page (2026) says "New child safety regulation is coming to Western Australia" and it is "designing a model tailored for Western Australia." No bill, commencement date or sector list yet (Communities page updated 21 Apr 2026).

**What this means for a club app.** Build to Principles 6, 8 and 10 now: a child-friendly, visible complaints pathway (Principle 6); communication controls, moderation and audit trails for all adult–child interaction in the app (Principle 8); and the ability to export policies, registers, training records and complaint logs for an auditor (Principle 10). Expect the Ombudsman's scheme to formalise these within the next 1–2 years.

## 4. Netball Australia and Netball WA child safeguarding — SPORT POLICY (binding by affiliation)

**Framework.** Netball Australia runs its *own* "Netball Integrity Policy Framework", drafted from Sport Integrity Australia (SIA) templates and "aligned with the Sport Integrity Australia policy standards" (NSW April 2024 update). I could not confirm Netball Australia on SIA's list of sports that formally adopted the National Integrity Framework (SIA site unreachable); the structure of its complaints policy (complaints handled by netball bodies at the level they occur, with NA/MO/Police referral for child abuse) indicates netball uses the *standards*, not SIA's independent complaint-handling service. Flag as unverified.

**Current policies** (netball.com.au, confirmed via Netball WA/QLD/NSW pages):
- **INT004 Safeguarding Children & Young People Policy**, effective **1 February 2025** (renamed from "Child Safeguarding Policy" INT011 in April 2024; adds conduct between young people and "Person in a Position of Authority"). The Feb 2025 PDF is image-only so clause text below is quoted from the 1 Jan 2023 version (INT011) and the Jan 2024 SIA template; the 2025 wording is expected to match the template but should be checked.
- **INT010 Member Protection Policy** (Safeguarding policy takes precedence over it since April 2024; Netball WA notes the MPP "is currently being updated").
- **INT001 Complaints & Disciplinary Policy v3**, effective **1 February 2026** (replaced the "Conduct & Disciplinary Policy").
- **Code of Conduct for Community Netball**, effective **1 February 2026** — applies to clubs and to "players, coaches, administrators, volunteers, parents and spectators", expressly "covers prohibited conduct occurring online", and makes individuals responsible for social-media posts and for "engaging with" (liking/sharing) offensive material.
Clubs are bound by affiliation; Netball WA: "As affiliates of Netball Australia, Netball WA, our Associations and Clubs are bound by Netball Australia's Integrity Framework."

**Key safeguarding requirements (Child Safe Practices annexure).**
- *Electronic communication*: 2023 netball wording — "For any electronic or online communication with Children in Netball we adopt a two-deep model, that is, copy in the organisation and a parent or guardian in all communication." SIA Jan 2024 template wording — a Person in a Position of Authority "must not communicate directly (one to one) with a Child/Young Person either electronically or online (including phone calls) without the inclusion of a representative from the Relevant Organisation and/or the Child/Young Person's parent or carer", except an Approved Person (the child's own parent/carer) or a Medical Practitioner/Health Professional whose communication "must only relate to appropriate and required medical care". Content must be "directly associated with delivering our services", "concise", "devoid of any sexualised language" and "not promoting unauthorised social activity or contact." Relevant Persons must not "have one on one contact with a Child outside of authorised sport activities (includes ... indirect, such as by phone, or online)".
- *Images*: prior written parental consent (may be "electronic messaging formats such as email or SMS"); context related to netball; appropriately dressed; taken in presence of other personnel; not distributed outside the organisation without parent approval; stored to prevent unauthorised access and deleted when no longer required; not published online or in print without written parental consent or de-identified; not retained on personal devices.
- *Supervision*: one-to-one unsupervised situations avoided; where unavoidable (e.g. physio treatment) they "will need to be identified and recorded" and any incident reported to management within 24 hours.
- *Recruitment & screening (Appendix 5)*: all roles assessed for child-relatedness; "All personnel that require a WWCC will supply a copy of it to, or be validated by, the organisation"; organisations "may not engage a person who does not have a satisfactory WWCC"; may mandate a WWCC where state law permits but does not require; serious breach if a person with disqualifying convictions is appointed.
- *Complaints (INT001 2026)*: each Relevant Organisation (including clubs) "must appoint a designated person (Complaints Manager)"; complaints lodged at the level where conduct occurred; "matters involving either Child Abuse or Serious Criminal Conduct ... must be referred immediately to NA, the Netball Organisation where the Affiliate is a Member, and Police"; complaints may be lodged by a parent for a Vulnerable Person, via the Netball Speak Up Hotline (1800 955 900, netballspeakup@coreintegrity.com.au); "Complaints made via social media are discouraged and will not be acknowledged or accepted." Netball WA (NWA-GUI-41, Sep 2025): "Do not attempt to resolve these matters within the Club or Association" — report to the NWA Complaints & Integrity Team via the NWA Complaints Form; Netball WA only intervenes for serious criminal conduct or child abuse.
- *Child Safety Officer*: the policy lists "appointing a Child Safety Officer" as an implementation measure; Netball WA requires each club to have "An appointed Working with Children Check Registration Officer", a categories-of-child-related-work record sheet, a roles record sheet and a WWC procedures document (NWA-POL-27 v2, reviewed July 2024, review due July 2026). Clubs must "maintain accurate records of all individuals with a current WWC Check and periodically check the validity"; expired card = no child-related activity until re-applied; NWA must be notified "in writing within a reasonable timeframe" of any INN/Negative Notice; NWA may audit.
- *MPIO*: Netball WA describes MPIOs as impartial first contacts and provides its own; no document located that *mandates* a club-level MPIO (Netball NSW 2026 memo assumes clubs have one). Flag: unverified as a hard requirement.
- *Education*: Netball WA points to the free Play by the Rules Child Protection course and SIA's Child Safeguarding in Sport Induction; community coaches must hold a WWC card and Foundation accreditation (Netball WA coach listing). No mandatory course was found in netball policy text; INT001 2026 gives organisations discretion to direct members to education.
- *Netball WA photography*: NWA-POL-19 Photography and Videography Policy governs images at NWA events.

**What this means for a club app.** The two-deep rule is the central design constraint: no adult-to-minor direct message channel; every message to an under-18 must automatically include a parent/guardian and/or a club representative, with an exception path for the child's own parent and for physios (health-only, logged). Block adult–minor "friend"/follow features. Images of minors need a consent record per child (parental, written/electronic), restricted distribution, retention/deletion, and a per-image publish flag. Provide a visible complaints entry point mapped to the club's Complaints Manager with immediate escalation path to Netball WA/Police for child-abuse matters, and keep decision/sanction records. Add committee-managed "Complaints Manager" and "WWC Registration Officer" roles.

## 5. Sport Integrity Australia — National Integrity Framework (SPORT POLICY / GUIDANCE)

**Status.** SIA remains a standalone Commonwealth agency (Health portfolio); no transfer of safeguarding functions to the Australian Sports Commission was found in 2025–26 (the Directory notes 2025 Administrative Arrangements Order updates but no functional change was located — flag as checked, not found). On **24 July 2026** SIA released the updated National Integrity Framework and Sport Integrity Policy Standards — "the biggest update since the Framework launched in 2021" — with sports required to adopt by **30 April 2027**. Five policies: **Safeguarding Children and Young People Policy; Participant Protection Policy** (renamed from Member Protection Policy); Complaints, Disputes and Discipline Policy; Competition Manipulation and Sport Wagering; Improper Use of Drugs and Medicine. Reported changes (SIA media statement; Football Australia Circular 26-01 of 27 Aug 2026): clearer separation of rules from recommendations via non-binding Companion Guides; a single "allegations" intake so people need not decide between a report and a complaint; stronger requirements for independent Complaint Managers and for allegations involving an NSO; an express obligation on NSOs to develop and implement **education plans**, with affiliates able to direct participants to SIA/ASC education "relevant and proportionate to their level of participation"; clarified initial evaluation, stayed investigations, provisional action and appeals. Netball Australia's adoption of the 2026 standards was not confirmed (its Feb 2026 policies pre-date the July release; expect an update before April 2027).

**Child Safeguarding Policy content** (Jan 2024 template, see section 4): Prohibited Conduct, mandatory Child/Young Person Safe Practices (two-deep electronic communication, images, supervision, transport, overnight stays, changing rooms), recruitment/screening, response procedure. SIA's "Dos and Don'ts – Digital communication" page (undated, c. 2024) states "a two-deep model must be adopted" and a person in authority "must not communicate one-to-one with a child, electronically or by phone, without including a club representative or the child's parent or carer." SIA's Photography and Filming of Children and Young People in Sport Guide and "Creating safer sport for children and young people" (National Principles guide, updated Jan 2026) are guidance.

**Complaints.** For sports that formally adopt the NIF, SIA handles safeguarding and discrimination complaints as the independent handler (only for conduct after adoption). Netball handles its own under INT001 (section 4). Safe Sport Hotline 1800 161 361; Sexual Misconduct Helpline 1800 272 435 (ausport clearinghouse, updated 27 Jul 2026).

**Education.** *Child Safeguarding in Sport Induction* (SIA eLearning, ~45 min, "developed for anyone bound by the policy, including ... clubs, coaches, officials and participants") and *Play by the Rules Child Protection* (free) are the courses netball bodies point to. Neither is mandated by WA law; whether netball mandates them is at the discretion of NA/NWA under INT001 cl 17. The *Safeguarding in Sport Continuous Improvement Program* (Recognise–Achieve–Embed) is an NSO-level program, not a club obligation.

**Could not verify directly:** all sportintegrity.gov.au pages (503). Facts above come from SIA PDFs mirrored elsewhere, the Football Australia circular, Snow Australia, ausport.gov.au and search excerpts.

**What this means for a club app.** Design the complaints module to the 2026 "single allegation intake" pattern and allow configuration of an independent Complaint Manager; record education completions per person (course, date, provider) because education plans and "direct to education" powers are now explicit; keep the two-deep communication model (unchanged in 2026 as far as sources show).

## 6. Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth) — LAW

**Privacy Act coverage.** An incorporated or unincorporated association is an "organisation" but is exempt as a small business operator if annual turnover is **$3 million or less**, unless it is a health service provider, trades in personal information, is a Commonwealth contractor, or opts in (OAIC "Rights and responsibilities"). A club physio who holds health records *may* make the club a "health service provider" for those records — obtain advice if the app stores injury/treatment notes. The *Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024* (assent 10 Dec 2024) did **not** remove the small-business exemption; the statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (in force 10 June 2025) applies regardless of turnover. The Government has agreed in principle to remove the exemption in "tranche 2" but no bill was found as at October 2026; from 1 July 2026 the exemption is partly withdrawn only for AML/CTF "designated service" providers. Vendor claims that the exemption "ends 1 July 2026" for everyone are wrong.

**Children's Online Privacy Code.** *Privacy (Children's Online Privacy) Code 2026* exposure draft released **31 March 2026**; consultation closed **5 June 2026**; must be registered by **10 December 2026**; commencement date "will be determined and inserted in the final published version" — not yet set as at 12 Oct 2026 (no registration notice found). It binds **APP entities** (so the small-business exemption still applies) that provide a social media service, relevant electronic service or designated internet service (Online Safety Act meanings) that is "likely to be accessed by children" or "primarily concerned with the activities of children" — the explanatory statement's examples include "family photo sharing applications" and school management systems, so a club app for juniors is squarely the kind of service contemplated if the operator is an APP entity. Obligations: reasonable steps to ascertain age (risk-based, or apply child protections to everyone); collect only what is strictly necessary and in the child's best interests; parental consent for under-15s with reasonable steps to verify parental responsibility; child-friendly privacy policy; notice of monitoring/geolocation; right of destruction; PIAs for high-risk processing.

**Social Media Minimum Age.** *Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024* obligations commenced **10 December 2025**: providers of "age-restricted social media platforms" must take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s holding accounts; penalties up to 150,000 penalty units (~$49.5m) for bodies corporate. The *Online Safety (Age-Restricted Social Media Platforms) Rules 2025* (made July 2025) exclude services whose "sole or primary purpose" is messaging/email/voice/video calling, online gaming, product/service information, professional networking, education, or health. eSafety's FAQ (positions as at 10 Dec 2025 and 25 Mar 2026) lists Discord, Messenger, WhatsApp, Google Classroom, YouTube Kids etc. as not currently age-restricted. There is no size threshold; classification turns on purpose and whether the service's significant purpose is online social interaction between users. A club management app whose primary purpose is team administration (fixtures, availability, club notices) with messaging as an ancillary or sole-purpose-communication feature is unlikely to be an age-restricted platform, but one with open user-generated feeds, profiles and following could be. Self-assess using eSafety's tool and document it. SIA notes clubs should factor in that many platforms now exclude under-16s.

**eSafety guidance for sport** (esafety.gov.au/communities/sport — site unreachable, from search excerpts and Vicsport report): "Never post member information (including names, videos, photos) without their consent"; use platforms with strong privacy settings and review them; include a senior member of the organisation in communications with participants; keep professional and personal social media separate; tell participants and parents where online communication will happen and how long photos/video are kept; collect evidence and report serious abuse to eSafety.

**What this means for a club app.** Confirm the operating entity's turnover and health-service status; if under $3m and no health records, the Privacy Act does not bind the club, but the Code's standards (age assurance, parental consent under 15, data minimisation, child-friendly notice, deletion rights) are the defensible baseline and will bind many vendors the club relies on. Keep the app's primary purpose clearly administrative, avoid open social feeds for minors, and document a Rules-based self-assessment that it is not an age-restricted social media platform.

## 7. WA-specific sport guidance — GUIDANCE

- **DLGSC became the Department of Creative Industries, Tourism and Sport (CITS) on 1 July 2025.** Its child protection page (reviewed 18 Nov 2025) says sport and recreation organisations "have responsibilities to comply with the Act and keep children safe", partners with SportWest, the WWC Screening Unit, Surf Life Saving WA and WA Police Child Abuse Squad, and links Play by the Rules. (It still cites "Working with Children Act (WA 2006)" — the Act is the 2004 Act; the page is out of date on the name.)
- **SportWest TrueSport Child Safeguarding** (state-funded) helps WA sport apply the ten National Principles; Netball WA references TrueSport's Reporting Flow Chart template.
- **CITS Child Safe Self-assessment Tool** — Netball WA says it "should be reviewed every year."
- **Commissioner for Children and Young People WA** — Child Safe Organisations WA Guidelines (Nov 2019), self-assessment tool, checklists for staff/volunteers and parents (section 3).
- **Department of Communities Child Safe Organisations Knowledge Hub** and the WWC "Compliance toolkit for sport and recreation" (ORG01), record-keeping spreadsheet and volunteer declaration (ORG04, updated Aug 2026).
- **Ombudsman WA Child Safe Standards scheme** — coming regulation for all organisations in child-related work (section 3).

**What this means for a club app.** Mirror the WWC toolkit's record-keeping spreadsheet fields (organisation/season, names as on card, DOB, role, card number, expiry, status, validation date; for exempt volunteers: position and confirmation of exemption) so an export satisfies a Screening Unit compliance check, a Netball WA audit, and the annual CITS/CCYP self-assessment.
